UX Club @ NYU // 05

// Formats

Three kinds of design challenge

Live · 45–60 min

Blue sky

An open prompt, no existing product. You drive the whole process; the interviewer mostly answers questions.

Shows: structure, curiosity, pace.

Live · 45–60 min

Collaborative

The interviewer plays your PM or teammate. They push back, add constraints and build with you.

Shows: listening, adapting, holding a position calmly.

Async or live

Take-home or critique

Design on your own time and present it, or critique an existing product out loud.

Shows: craft, judgment, a clear story.

Tonight: blue sky, with a classmate playing the interviewer.

// What it is

They’re grading your thinking, out loud.

A prompt you’ve never seen. 45–60 minutes. A marker, a whiteboard and an interviewer playing your stakeholder. The polish of the final sketch matters least.

UX Club @ NYU // 11

// Step 01

Questions worth asking first

Who

Who is this for, and who is it not for?

Problem

What’s frustrating about how they do this today?

Context

Where and when will they use it?

Constraints

Any limits on platform, budget or time?

Success

What would make this a win for the business?

Scope

Is this new, or part of an existing product?

Then commit: “I’ll assume ___. Tell me if that’s off.”

UX Club @ NYU // 12

// Pitfalls

Common traps, and what to do instead

The trapTry this instead
Jumping straight to screensSpend the first minutes on who and why.
Designing for “everyone”Pick one user. Mention others later.
Drawing in silenceNarrate what you’re doing and why.
Trying to solve everythingChoose one core flow and go deep.
Treating pushback as attackTreat it as input: “Good point, here’s how I’d adjust.”
Running out of timeSave the last 8–10 minutes for the walkthrough.

UX Club @ NYU // 13

// The overlap

Balance the user and the business

User needs

Business goals

Your design

Name both, out loud. “The user wants X; the business needs Y.”

Give each a metric. Time to complete a task; retention or revenue.

When they clash, pick a side and explain why.

e.g. a grocery app: users want to reorder in seconds; the business wants bigger baskets. One-tap reorder with a “running low?” nudge serves both.

UX Club @ NYU // 17

// Teams

Two roles on every team

1 per team

Interviewer

Holds the card and answers the team’s questions. Doesn’t design. Gives feedback after the share-out.

Everyone else

Designers

Work through the challenge together. Ask questions, think out loud and sketch on the whiteboard.

One rule: everyone holds the marker at least once, and everyone speaks in the share-out.
